I'm a PhD student in mathematics at [Louisiana State University](https://www.math.lsu.edu/), where I work with Jerome Hoffman, Fang-Ting Tu, and Scott Baldridge. I work on the algebraic and arithmetic geometry of curves: their covers, their moduli, and the parallel questions over rings of integers. I'm also interested in the $p$-adic cohomology theories of mixed-characteristic geometry and in the homotopical methods that now underlie them.

My current work studies K-trivial covers of a curve $X$: finite flat covers $Y \to X$ with trivial dualizing sheaf. With a trivialization, such a cover is the same as a commutative Frobenius algebra sheaf on $X$ whose trace takes values in the canonical bundle, that is, a two-dimensional TQFT twisted by $\omega_X$. On a curve of genus at least two these covers are never reduced and never smoothable, and their moduli turn out to be classical objects: theta characteristics, spin curves and their Hodge bundles, Prym varieties and Brill&ndash;Noether loci. A first paper proves a structure theorem in every degree. A sequel classifies the covers of degree four and describes their moduli stack. A third paper carries the same dictionary to rings of integers, where the inverse different replaces the canonical bundle and the questions become ones about Steinitz classes, capitulation in the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_2$-tower, and Selmer groups.

My master's thesis studies syntomic cohomology and its relation to prismatic cohomology and topological cyclic homology. Separately, with Fang-Ting Tu and Jerome Hoffman, I construct genus-2 curves whose Jacobians split, through explicit elliptic subcovers.

I received a B.A. in Mathematics and Physics (double major) from [Johns Hopkins University](https://www.jhu.edu/) in 2025, where I wrote my first paper with Mee Seong Im and Mikhail Khovanov.

Research interests
======

* **Algebraic and arithmetic geometry of curves:** moduli of curves and of their covers; spin curves, theta characteristics and Prym varieties; class groups and Selmer groups
* **$p$-adic Hodge theory:** syntomic and prismatic cohomology
* **Homotopy theory:** functorial field theories; motivic homotopy theory and hermitian K-theory; trace methods (topological Hochschild and cyclic homology); formal moduli problems
