I’m a PhD student in mathematics at [Louisiana State University](https://www.math.lsu.edu/), where I work with Jerome Hoffman, Fang-Ting Tu, and Scott Baldridge. My research sits between arithmetic geometry and homotopy theory.

My current projects study self-dual algebraic structures (Frobenius algebras, or equivalently two-dimensional TQFTs) over curves and over rings of integers. On a curve these structures are controlled by torsion in the Jacobian and by spin structures; over a number field they are controlled by the class group, and following them up towers of number fields leads to Iwasawa theory and syntomic cohomology. In a related project I construct genus-2 curves whose Jacobians split, through explicit elliptic subcovers.

The larger picture I am working toward is the Langlands program seen through topology. Number fields behave like three-manifolds, and recent work recasts automorphic periods and $L$-functions as boundary data for topological field theories. I am interested in how far that analogy can be made precise, and in the homotopical tools it calls for: derived and formal moduli problems, motivic and chromatic homotopy theory, and algebraic K-theory.

I received a B.A. in Mathematics and a B.S. in Physics from [Johns Hopkins University](https://www.jhu.edu/) in 2025.

Research interests
======

* **Arithmetic geometry:** curves, Jacobians and Prym varieties; class groups and Iwasawa theory; $p$-adic Hodge theory and syntomic cohomology
* **Homotopy theory:** motivic and chromatic homotopy theory, algebraic K-theory, higher algebra
* **Algebraic geometry:** moduli and deformation theory, formal moduli problems
* **Langlands program:** automorphic forms and theta correspondences; field-theoretic approaches to Langlands duality
