The homotopy groups of spheres are among the simplest objects to define in algebraic topology and among the hardest to compute. This note uses them as a guide through covering spaces, cellular approximation, fibrations and their long exact sequences, and suspension, showing what each technique computes. Along the way it proves that $\pi_n(S^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, that $\pi_3(S^2) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ is generated by the Hopf map, and that $\pi_i(S^4) \cong \pi_i(S^7) \oplus \pi_{i-1}(S^3)$.

The Freudenthal suspension theorem is proved from homotopy excision and used to introduce stable homotopy groups, which are shown to form a generalized homology theory. The note closes with an introduction to stable homotopy theory: the Spanier–Whitehead category and spectra, the Pontryagin–Thom description of the stems by framed manifolds, the Hopf invariant and the J-homomorphism, the Adams spectral sequence, and the chromatic picture.
