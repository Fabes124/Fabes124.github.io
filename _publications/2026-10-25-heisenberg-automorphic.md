The right regular representation of the $(2n+1)$-dimensional Heisenberg group $H$ on $L^2(\Gamma\backslash H)$, with $\Gamma$ its integer points, is the simplest nonabelian instance of the representation-theoretic approach to automorphic forms, and unlike the reductive case it can be decomposed completely. For $m \neq 0$, the part on which the center acts by $e^{2\pi i m z}$ is a sum of $|m|^n$ copies of the Schrödinger representation, with explicit theta series as intertwiners (the Weil–Brezin map when $m = 1$).

The note derives classical consequences from this decomposition: quasi-periodicity of theta functions as the Heisenberg group law, Poisson summation and the transformation law $\vartheta(-1/\tau) = \sqrt{-i\tau}\,\vartheta(\tau)$ from multiplicity one, and the dimension of the space of level-$m$ theta functions. It then explains the multiplicity $|m|^n$ in two further ways: via a finite Heisenberg group acting on the multiplicity space, and adelically, where each Schrödinger representation occurs once and the factor $|m|^n$ comes from the finite places.
